# Chip Technology: 180-Day Learning Curriculum

> Six learning months of 30 study days each. This is an ordered learning plan, not a promise of calendar-day completion. Start at zero, learn by doing, and publish each day after testing.

## Daily publishing workflow

1. Read the day's topic and inspect the illustration.
2. Complete `notes.md`, `practical.py` and `questions.md` as appropriate.
3. Run the practical and tests; capture what worked and what failed.
4. Add a unique diagram in `images/` and reference it in notes.
5. Commit the day's implementation to GitHub. Do not mark unfinished work complete.

**Status:** Days 001–010 have existing lessons; Days 011–180 are a planned syllabus until their exercises are created and tested.

## Month Overview

- **[Month 01 — Semiconductor and Digital Logic Foundations](month-01-chip-fundamentals/README.md)** — What Is a Computer Chip? through Month 1 Mini Project: Logic Toolkit
- **[Month 02 — Sequential Circuits, Timing, and Memory](month-02-digital-circuits/README.md)** — Review of Combinational Building Blocks through Month 2 Mini Project: 8-Bit ALU
- **[Month 03 — Verilog, RTL Design, and Verification](month-03-verilog-rtl/README.md)** — Install or Choose a Lightweight Verilog Simulator through Month 3 Mini Project: Verified ALU RTL
- **[Month 04 — Computer Architecture and RISC-V](month-04-riscv-cpu/README.md)** — CPU Datapath Overview through Month 4 Mini Project: Tiny RISC-V CPU Model
- **[Month 05 — AI Hardware and Accelerator Fundamentals](month-05-ai-accelerators/README.md)** — Why AI Needs Specialized Hardware through Month 5 Mini Project: 2x2 AI MAC Array
- **[Month 06 — AI Accelerator Capstone and Portfolio](month-06-capstone/README.md)** — Define the Accelerator Specification through Final Review and Release v1.0
